# Where the game stands, and what we must, can and will improve (November 2026, round 2)

Follows `docs/RESEARCH_2026_11.md`. New research: the two closest commercial rivals, AI design notes, event design, accessibility, menus, Godot rendering. Several searches returned little (marked); grades are in the page.


## Done since the first research

* **Spread / Muster, undo, alert settings, breakdown tooltips, outliner, people panel, state works, AI aims, release vassal, lenses.** Everything from the first research list that you approved is built (version 1.6.0). Verified by tests; not yet on a real device.
* **Title entrance and motion.** Both stages now come in with an entrance (wordmark and rows in turn, globe fades up and settles from a fast spin) and the title switches the project's low-processor mode off while it moves. A pixel test checks that both stages change on screen at 1280x720 and 2340x1080. I could not reproduce "no animation at all" on this machine, so see the open point below.

## Must (before the next release)

* **Check 1.6.0 on a real phone and a real Windows machine.** Nothing has run on hardware since 1.4.0. The things most likely to differ from my test machine: the title (see below), memory of the 5120x2560 globe, frame rate of the title at full speed, the size of the new numbers in every window, the new Decrees section and the Outliner at phone width. Sources: [Godot docs on custom drawing (queue_redraw every frame for continuous change)](https://docs.godotengine.org/de/4.5/tutorials/2d/custom_drawing_in_2d.html)
* **Settle the "no animation" report.** On this machine both stages move (pixel test). On yours they did not. Possible causes in order of likelihood: Reduce motion is on (Settings > Comfort), Effects is Off (Settings > Display), the screen only redraws on request (low-processor mode: fixed now), or a driver that throttles. Please look at those two settings first; if both are as expected and 1.6.0 still stands still, tell me the device and I will add an on-screen motion diagnostic. Sources: [Godot docs on custom drawing (queue_redraw every frame for continuous change)](https://docs.godotengine.org/de/4.5/tutorials/2d/custom_drawing_in_2d.html); [Game Developer, "Setting the tone: main menus are the game"](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/setting-the-tone-main-menus-are-the-game)
* **A native read of the new Russian and Uzbek text.** About 150 new strings since 1.4.0 were written by me (alerts, works, aims, codex, lenses, outliner). A native speaker must read them.
* **A first-ten-turns guide for rules 5.** Rules 5 changed how a game starts (army in the capital, people at scale). The briefing, the tutorial and the advisor mention it, but a short objective list for the first ten turns of any scenario is still missing (research item U5). Sources: [Our first research page](https://claude.ai/artifact/AgGmX3DQqWcPbgSFy6owDn)

## Can (good value, not urgent)

* **AI personalities with a plan, not only a number.** CK3's AI is sorted into archetypes: warlike states keep a war chest first, cautious ones prepare to be attacked. Ours have an aggression number. We can give each personality a visible habit: warlike saves gold and recruits before declaring, cautious fortifies the front, merchants buy trade and works first. It makes wars readable and the aims we just added more believable. Sources: [CK3 Dev Diary #104, AI personalities (Paradox)](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/ck3-dev-diary-104-ai-ai-ai.1539411/)
* **Large, paced events (an AI director).** Event-driven design keeps a grand strategy game different each time, but only large events carry replayability and someone must pace them. We have about forty historical and a set of random events. We can add chains (an event that sets up the next) and a small director that spaces big events and avoids repeating a theme. Sources: [Game Wisdom, event-driven game design](https://game-wisdom.com/critical/event-driven-game-design)
* **Infrastructure that moves armies.** Supremacy 1914 ties output and movement to infrastructure and railways; in our game "Great Roads" now cuts administration, but armies still walk one province a turn everywhere. A later-era railway work that lets a march cross two provinces would give the industrial eras their own feel. Sources: [MMO Reviews / MMOBomb on Supremacy 1914](https://mmobomb.com/review/supremacy-1914)
* **Keyboard remapping and a shortcut sheet.** The accessibility guidelines list remappable controls among the basic items. We have scaling, contrast, colour-blind, reduced motion, spoken text and confirm settings; keys are fixed (F1-F7, Ctrl+Z, S, R, M...). Sources: [Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/)
* **Quick game and fast-forward for AI turns.** A turn takes seconds to a minute; a "quick game" preset and a skippable turn summary would suit phones.

## Will (proposed releases)

* **1.7: content and readability.** First-ten-turns guide, AI archetype habits, event chains and the director, keyboard remapping. Smaller: after-action battle card, explaining why the AI said no (item U6/U7 of the first page).
* **1.8: scenarios that end.** Era campaigns with named objectives and an ending screen with the statistics we already record; a second look at victory paths that are too easy or too slow in each era (the audit found tech wins at turn 40 in Napoleonic).
* **2.0: multiplayer.** The server and lockstep protocol exist and pass their test; the game is "coming soon" in releases. The research on Supremacy and Conflict of Nations is a warning: real-time play with premium currency draws pay-to-win complaints and "can't pause" frustration. Our answer should be turn-based, simultaneous orders, no purchases. Sources: [Pocket Tactics on Conflict of Nations: WW3](https://www.pockettactics.com/conflict-of-nations-world-war-3/review); [MMO Reviews / MMOBomb on Supremacy 1914](https://mmobomb.com/review/supremacy-1914)

## Lessons worth keeping in mind

* **Do not ship pay-to-win.** The most common criticism of the two closest commercial rivals is that money buys morale, speed or resources, and that you cannot pause. We are offline, turn-based and free of purchases: keep it that way and say so on the store page. Sources: [Pocket Tactics on Conflict of Nations: WW3](https://www.pockettactics.com/conflict-of-nations-world-war-3/review)
* **Pops are expensive; headcount is not.** Victoria 3 found that performance depends on the NUMBER of pops, not the number of people, and spent a patch limiting how migration splits them. Our people are one number per province, which is why a 30:1 scale costs nothing. If we ever add classes or religions, add them as shares of a province, not as more objects. Sources: [Victoria 3 Dev Diary #105, migration and pop counts (Paradox)](https://admin-forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/victoria-3-dev-diary-105-migration-in-1-6.1624226)
* **A menu sets the tone.** Menus that carry the genre and a "warm welcome" are remembered; subtle animation gives feedback. This is why both title stages now have an entrance rather than appearing at once. Sources: [Game Developer, "Setting the tone: main menus are the game"](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/setting-the-tone-main-menus-are-the-game)
* **Low-processor mode needs a redraw request.** Godot only redraws what asks to be redrawn when the project sleeps between frames; continuous change must call queue_redraw every frame or switch the mode off. We now do both on the title. Sources: [Godot docs on custom drawing (queue_redraw every frame for continuous change)](https://docs.godotengine.org/de/4.5/tutorials/2d/custom_drawing_in_2d.html)
